\subsection{A closed-form condition for ranking inversion}\label{sec:m-law}
Stratifying the leaky test set by maximum similarity to training into a leaked stratum (similarity $\ge0.9$, fraction $\varphi$) and a novel stratum ($<0.5$) gives, for any model $M$ with leaked-stratum accuracy $h_M$ and novel-stratum accuracy $n_M$, the two-stratum identity $a_M=n_M+\varphi g_M$ with $g_M=h_M-n_M$ the graded memorization gap of \S4.4, so the split-induced drop is the product $\Delta_M=\varphi g_M$. For an ordered pair $(A,B)$ with $A$ the leader on the as-shipped split, writing $\delta=n_B-n_A$ for $B$'s true novel-sequence advantage and $\Delta g=g_A-g_B$ for $A$'s excess memorization propensity, the as-shipped margin is $\mu=a_A-a_B=\varphi\Delta g-\delta$. The benchmark therefore reports the wrong winner exactly when $0<\delta<\varphi\Delta g$, i.e.\ when $\varphi$ exceeds the \emph{ranking breakdown point}
\begin{equation}\varphi^{*}=\delta/\Delta g.\label{eq:phistar}\end{equation}
We stress that the algebraically equivalent form $0<\mu<\Delta_A-\Delta_B$ carries \emph{no} predictive content: substituting $\Delta_M=a_M-a_M^{\mathrm{corr}}$ reduces it to $a_B^{\mathrm{corr}}>a_A^{\mathrm{corr}}$, which is the definition of a corrected-split inversion restated. We compute it only as an arithmetic-consistency check on our own tables, where it must hold $12/12$ by algebra. All predictive content lies in \eqref{eq:phistar}, whose inputs $(\varphi,n,g)$ are measured on the as-shipped split alone and never touch the corrected split. Uncertainty on $\delta$ and on the inversion margin $m_{\mathrm{inv}}=\varphi\Delta g-\delta$ comes from the same cluster (block) bootstrap used in \S4.9, resampling whole within-test near-duplicate components, $10{,}000$ replicates; a pair is \textsc{clean}-inverting when both CIs exclude zero and \textsc{fragile} when the $\delta$ CI straddles zero.

\subsection{Coordinate-space construction signatures}\label{sec:m-coord}
Genomic Benchmarks ships per-sequence intervals (\texttt{id,region,start,end,strand}) separately from the sequences its loader returns. We recover them offline and realign them to loader row order, which is deterministic (\texttt{split}, then sorted class directory, then sorted filename, the filename stem being the interval \texttt{id}); recovered interval width equals sequence length for $100\%$ of rows in all eight datasets, and where the extracted files are present the recovered order matches the true filenames exactly. Because these coordinates are causally upstream of, and statistically independent from, the $k$-mer clustering that measures leakage, they furnish a genuinely cross-modal test rather than a restatement. Per class we compute self-overlap redundancy $R_{\mathrm{self}}=1-n_{\mathrm{merged}}/n_{\mathrm{raw}}$, the share of intervals sharing $\ge t$ reciprocal overlap with another interval of the same class, and the share of \emph{test} intervals overlapping any \emph{train} interval at $\ge t$ reciprocal overlap, for $t\in\{{\ge}1\,\mathrm{bp},0.5,0.9,1.0\}$. Overlap is strand-agnostic throughout, since strand is unrecorded for \textit{human\_ocr\_ensembl} and \textit{human\_ensembl\_regulatory}. The construction rule thresholds the maximum over classes of the $\ge50\%$ reciprocal cross-split statistic at $0.1$---the same cut already used for the sequence-space verdict---so it introduces no fitted parameter.

\subsection{Construct-and-break manipulations}\label{sec:m-manip}
To test the construction rule causally rather than correlationally we intervene on the construction step alone. \emph{Break-a-clean}: \textit{human\_ocr\_ensembl}, drawn from the merged Ensembl Regulatory Build and clean by every measure, is rebuilt the way an un-merged multi-sample union is built, by duplicating the same $94.3\%$ share of positive rows that we measure on \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl}. \emph{Fix-a-leaky}: \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl} receives the merge step its curators omitted---every duplicated coordinate collapsed to a single representative row---with sequences, labels, model code and split ratio unchanged. Each manipulation is paired with an unmanipulated control run of the identical pipeline at the same scale and split seed, so any difference is attributable to the intervention rather than to re-splitting or model variance.

\subsection{Cross-suite leak census}\label{sec:m-census}
We apply the census step of \S4.1 unchanged to the Nucleotide Transformer downstream tasks, which exist in two releases by the same authors: the original and a revised release whose card states that it "replaced randomly generated negative samples by existing chunks of genomes" and gave "all datasets proper chromosome held-out test sets." Because the two releases share task names, the pair isolates curation from biology. Training sets are capped at $20{,}000$ sequences; capping can only remove potential near-duplicate partners, so every reported fraction is a lower bound---the same direction of error that makes our own \textit{demo\_coding} verdict tighten, not loosen, at full scale.

\subsection{The \texttt{certify} protocol}\label{sec:m-certify}
We package the audit as an executable standard with eight checks: run at full scale; report both length-blind and length-robust leak fractions; confirm with an alignment engine; include a memorization-prone model as a tripwire; certify on the graded gap and label concordance rather than accuracy; run a matched random re-split control; put cluster-bootstrap intervals with ICC and design effect on every delta; and, where coordinates ship, apply the construction rule of \S\ref{sec:m-coord}. A green verdict carries the heavier evidential burden: a dataset is certified clean only when every applicable check passes, whereas one failing check flags it. The tripwire may escalate a verdict only when the high-similarity bin exceeds the $n\ge50$ confidence floor used in \S4.4 \emph{and} the homology-corrected drop exceeds the matched random re-split drop, so that ordinary repartitioning cost is never mistaken for leakage.

\subsection{A closed-form condition predicts when leakage reorders a ranking}\label{sec:r-law}
% inversion_law_{anchors,pairs,winner,regpath,inject,critical_f,bootstrap}.csv
The two-stratum identity is accurate enough to be used predictively: it reconstructs as-shipped accuracy to a mean absolute error of $0.0020$ across the four models on \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl} and $0.0175$ on \textit{human\_nontata\_promoters}, the difference being fully explained by mid-similarity mass the two-stratum form ignores ($1.4\%$ of the test set on the former, $22.4\%$ on the latter; the exact multi-bin identity holds to $10^{-4}$ on both). Applied to the marquee pair, $\delta=+0.0122$ and $\Delta g=0.1929$ give a breakdown point $\varphi^{*}=0.063$ against a measured $\varphi=0.380$---a sixfold exceedance---so \eqref{eq:phistar} predicts the observed random-forest-to-LinearSVC inversion, with cluster-bootstrap CIs excluding zero for both $\delta$ ($[0.0054,0.0192]$) and $m_{\mathrm{inv}}$ ($[0.0560,0.0663]$): \textsc{clean}. On \textit{human\_nontata\_promoters} every random-forest pair has $\delta\le0$, so the same inequality predicts \emph{no} genuine inversion, and no pair there is \textsc{clean}. The condition thus reproduces from a single inequality the airtight-versus-cautionary distinction that \S4.3 established case by case. Out of sample, the law predicts the corrected-split winner on $4/4$ metric readouts for \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl} and $3/4$ for \textit{human\_nontata\_promoters}, the sole disagreement being the accuracy readout that \S4.3 independently flags as a dead heat. Its one assumption, $a^{\mathrm{corr}}\approx n$, is imperfect in an informative way: retraining on the de-leaked set costs the memorizer far more than the others (residual $-0.074$ for the random forest on \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl} versus $+0.012$ to $+0.016$ for the other three), so the law \emph{under}-predicts how far the forest falls. It is therefore a sufficient, not necessary, condition---and across all twelve pairs it produced no false inversion call.

\subsection{The law forecasts the regularization path and the injection dose-response}\label{sec:r-oos}
% inversion_law_regpath.csv, inversion_law_inject.csv, inversion_law_critical_f.csv
Two sweeps already in hand test the condition out of sample with no fitted parameters. Along the $g$-axis, predicting the forest's as-shipped rank at each of the $22$ regularization cells of \S4.5 from $(\varphi,n,g)$ alone---$\varphi$ measured once per dataset, the three competitors fixed---matches the observed rank in $21/22$ cells ($11/11$ on \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl}, mean reconstruction error $0.0025$; $10/11$ on \textit{human\_nontata\_promoters}), and places the predicted crossover in $\texttt{min\_samples\_leaf}\in(5,20]$, exactly where it is observed. The hyperparameter objection is thereby answered constructively: we do not merely report that the sweep changes the result, we predict the sweep. Along the $\varphi$-axis, the injected-leakage construction of \S4.13 has a leak fraction known by construction, $\varphi(f)=n_{\mathrm{inj}}/(n_{\mathrm{test}}+n_{\mathrm{inj}})$. Fitting the leaked-stratum accuracy at $f=0.1$ only yields $h_{\mathrm{RF}}=0.9988$---the memorization ceiling, predicted \emph{a priori} to be $\approx1$ for near-exact copies---and $h_{\mathrm{LR}}=0.7096$, both invariant in $f$; the held-out cells are then predicted to within $0.0011$ ($f=0.2$) and $0.0008$ ($f=0.4$). The implied breakdown point $\varphi^{*}=0.041$ corresponds to a critical injection $f^{*}=0.011$, and the observed random-forest-over-logistic-regression flip indeed occurs between $f=0$ and $f=0.1$, bracketing it. Contaminating a clean benchmark with roughly one percent of its own training data suffices to change which model wins.

\subsection{Coordinate geometry predicts which datasets leak}\label{sec:r-coord}
% construction_signatures.csv, construction_rule.csv, construction_provenance.csv
The maximum-over-classes cross-split coordinate overlap separates the suite perfectly: the two leaky datasets sit at $0.755$ and $0.992$, the six others at $0.073$ and below---a $10.3\times$ gap straddling the inherited $0.1$ threshold, giving $8/8$ agreement with the sequence-space verdict at zero fitted cost. The decisive comparison is internal to Ensembl, which holds provider and organism constant so that only the merge step differs: \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl}, the raw FANTOM5 union over $808$ CAGE libraries, ships $77{,}421$ positives on only $40{,}934$ unique coordinates, with $94.3\%$ of positive rows sitting on a duplicated coordinate and $75.5\%$ of test positives having their exact coordinate present in training; \textit{human\_ocr\_ensembl} and \textit{human\_ensembl\_regulatory}, drawn from the merged "MultiCell" Regulatory Build, have self-overlap of exactly $0.000$ in every class. Every one of the $36{,}487$ duplicated-coordinate groups carries byte-identical sequences and a single label, so the coordinate redundancy is exactly the sequence redundancy. Quantitatively, the coordinate-predicted leak fraction for \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl} is $0.3776$ against a measured sequence leak of $0.3802$---agreement to $0.26$ percentage points, from two independent modalities. Two refinements were forced on us by the data and both matter. First, redundancy is not always in the positive class: \textit{human\_nontata\_promoters} carries essentially all of its redundancy in the \emph{negative} class ($R_{\mathrm{self}}=0.961$ versus $0.047$ for positives), so a positive-class-only statistic misses it entirely. Second, mere adjacency over-calls: \textit{drosophila\_enhancers\_stark} tiles at $2142$\,bp median length with base-pair-scale touches, giving $0.406$ at ${\ge}1$\,bp but $0.036$ at $\ge50\%$ reciprocal overlap, so without the reciprocal criterion the rule would produce a false positive. The two leaky datasets moreover leak by \emph{different} coordinate mechanisms---exact duplication on \textit{human\_enhancers\_ensembl} ($0.755$ exact) versus contiguous window tiling on \textit{human\_nontata\_promoters} ($0.992$ at $\ge50\%$, but $0.000$ exact)---while \textit{demo\_coding\_vs\_intergenomic\_seqs} is coordinate-clean ($0.006$) yet containment-borderline, its leakage being coding-paralog homology with no coordinate signature at all. Coordinates can therefore escalate a verdict but must never clear one, and the sequence detectors remain necessary.

\subsection{An independently built suite carries the same defect, and its own revision removes it}\label{sec:r-census}
% crosssuite_census.csv
Applying the identical census to the Nucleotide Transformer downstream tasks shows the phenomenon is not confined to Genomic Benchmarks. Of $13$ tasks in the original release, four are leaky and three borderline; \textit{enhancers} and \textit{enhancers\_types} carry $25.0\%$ test-to-train leakage that is \emph{byte-identical}, and \textit{splice\_sites\_acceptors} and \textit{\_donors} $0.244$ and $0.245$ at Jaccard $0.7$. We verified the enhancer figure directly: of $400$ test sequences, exactly $100$ are byte-identical to a training sequence, with label concordance $1.000$, and the training set itself holds $14{,}968$ rows on $14{,}002$ unique sequences. In the authors' own revised release, all $13$ tasks are clean, the largest leak fraction across the entire suite falling to $0.0020$ and exact duplication to zero. Task names, biology and authorship are held constant and only curation changed, so this is a controlled natural experiment---and it is the remedy this paper advocates, validated by a third party who did not set out to test it. Two of our pre-registered expectations failed and we report them as such: we predicted the original release would be leaky as a \emph{suite}, whereas its promoter tasks are clean ($0.005$--$0.015$). That failure is itself informative---the leakage of \textit{human\_nontata\_promoters} at $0.406$ is therefore not a property of the promoter task but of Genomic Benchmarks' construction of it, which separates task effect from curation effect and corroborates \S\ref{sec:r-coord}. Leakage is a property of particular constructions, not of suites or of subject matter. We emphasise the scope of this result: it is a leak \emph{census}, establishing that the precondition for ranking inversion is widespread, and not yet a demonstrated re-ranking on the second suite, which requires the model runs we defer to future work.

\subsection{The audit runs as an executable standard}\label{sec:r-certify}
% certify_self_validation.csv, certify_report_stark.json
\texttt{certify --self-validate} recomposes this paper's eight dataset verdicts from committed intermediate results and reproduces all eight, exiting non-zero on any drift; it is the repository's regression gate. The end-to-end path is executed rather than merely specified: on \textit{drosophila\_enhancers\_stark} it agrees with MMseqs2 on the verdict, drives residual leak after re-splitting to $0.000$, and returns a random-forest drop of $0.024$ with cluster-bootstrap CI $[0.002,0.043]$, ICC $1.00$ and design effect $1.004$. The protocol also caught a defect in itself, which we report because it illustrates why the green-verdict burden is asymmetric: an early version escalated this clean dataset to borderline on a memorizer gap of $0.197$ computed over a high-similarity bin of only ten sequences, below the confidence floor of \S4.4, while the matched random re-split control showed its drop was repartitioning cost rather than homology. Requiring both a populated bin and an excess over that control before the tripwire may fire restores the correct clean verdict.
